Swiss Ledger Presentation Overview:
The meeting introduced Dr. Lorenzo Barisone, a data scientist and project lead from the city of Lugano, who will present on the Swiss Ledger. Maciej, representing SPO Software, hosted the session and provided an overview of the Linux Foundation's Decentralized Trust initiative and the European chapter's role in promoting institutional adoption of decentralized technologies. The meeting will follow a 30-40 minute presentation by Lorenzo, followed by a Q&A session, and will conclude with member updates and announcements. The recording will be available on the LF Decentralized Trust YouTube channel the following day.

## Lugano's Blockchain Evolution Overview:
Lorenzo presented on the blockchain initiatives in Lugano, highlighting the city's issuance of the Luga payment token in 2020, which is now used by 40,000 users at over 500 merchants. He explained the evolution of the 3A chain into Swiss Ledger, a public permission blockchain network with more than 30 supporting entities, including public, private, and academic institutions. The network uses a proof of authority consensus mechanism and is built on the Ethereum virtual machine, anchored to Bitcoin and Ethereum for additional verifiability. Swiss Ledger has various applications, including a tokenization certification platform for physical art and KYB/KYC services, as well as public sector applications like the Luga municipal payment token and Verify Lugano for document certification.

## Swiss Open Banking Blockchain Initiative:
Lorenzo explained the Swiss approach to open banking, noting that while there is no mandatory enforcement, the regulatory framework is advanced. He discussed the lack of private platforms for open banking in Switzerland and proposed using blockchain technology to address privacy concerns. Lorenzo outlined the project's roadmap, which includes moving from pilot and proof of concept to early adoption in niche use cases, with plans for nationwide adoption and international connectivity. He also mentioned the creation of a financial innovation lab in Lugano, involving collaborations with technology providers and academic institutions to advance blockchain applications in banking and finance.

## Lugano's Blockchain and Currency Initiatives:
Lorenzo presented on Lugano's involvement with Swiss Ledger, highlighting their role as the first municipality to join the Swiss Blockchain Federation, which includes private companies sponsored by five Swiss cantons. He detailed Lugano's scientific efforts, including publications and presentations at international conferences, to provide an academic foundation for their blockchain initiatives. Maciej thanked Lorenzo for the presentation and introduced a Q&A session, encouraging participants to use the chat or raise hands for questions. Alfonso inquired about the relationship between the Luga, a community currency, and the WIR, an older communal currency, prompting Lorenzo to explain the political decision behind Lugano's initiatives and their potential replication in other Swiss cities.

## Luga Multi-Asset Wallet Overview:
Lorenzo explained that Luga is a multi-asset wallet that allows users to pay with Luga, Bitcoin, Tether, and Ethereum in local shops, restaurants, museums, and even for municipal taxes in Lugano. He clarified that the main users range from 18 to 94 years old, with a concentration between 20 and 40 years old. Lorenzo also detailed that users receive a 10% cashback when paying with Luga, and transactions are visible on the blockchain explorer, which is publicly accessible. Shane inquired about the blockchain's EVM and permission network, but Lorenzo did not provide a direct answer.

## Swiss Ledger: Privacy and Transparency:
Lorenzo explained that the Swiss Ledger is built on Ethereum Virtual Machine by Noku and is transparent with public protocols available on GitHub. He clarified that while transactions are public, users are identified by cryptographic identifiers, and the platform offers three KYC levels to manage privacy and transaction capabilities. Lorenzo confirmed that the Swiss Ledger is open to private companies, with 23 out of 36 supporting entities being private, subject to legal and technical requirements. Maciej noted that the meeting was closing, and Lorenzo provided his contact information for further questions.
